## Why cork suits the Australian way of living

**Natural materials for indoor and outdoor flow.** Australian design often blurs the line between inside and out, with timber, stone, linen and rattan. Cork belongs to the same family of honest, tactile materials, and it adds an organic sculptural note that complements them all.

**Texture that comes alive in bright light.** Strong natural light can flatten smooth, glossy surfaces. Genuine cork bark, with its fissures and deep grain, does the opposite: sunlight reveals every detail and casts soft shadows across the surface.

**Sustainability that matters.** Australians are increasingly conscious of where their furniture comes from. Cork is harvested by hand from the cork oak without cutting the tree down, and the bark regrows over nine years. It is natural, renewable and recyclable.

## How to use cork decor in an Australian home

- **Coastal homes.** Pair a sculptural cork bowl such as Abyss or Shell with white walls, pale timber and linen for a calm, beach-house feeling.
- **Open-plan living.** A tall cork sculpture such as Fireflies creates a vertical focal point in large living areas without blocking light or views.
- **Evening light.** After a bright day, cork table lamps such as Leaf or Shale give a warm, golden glow that makes interiors feel intimate.
- **Dining and entertaining.** A cork and glass table such as Alexis, or a cork and solid wood piece such as Granada, brings natural warmth to the heart of the home.

## Caring for cork in a sunny climate

Genuine cork is light, durable and naturally resistant to moisture and wear. To keep a piece looking its best in an Australian home:

1. Avoid placing it in prolonged direct sunlight, as with any natural material and fine furniture.
2. Dust gently with a dry cloth, and avoid chemical cleaning products.
3. In case of spills, absorb liquid immediately with a dry cloth.
4. Keep decorative pieces dry inside; they are not designed to hold liquids.
