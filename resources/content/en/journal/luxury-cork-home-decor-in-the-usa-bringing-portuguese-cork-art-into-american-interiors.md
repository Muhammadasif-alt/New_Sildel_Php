American interior design is turning towards natural, honest materials. Designers from New York to Los Angeles are replacing glossy, mass-produced finishes with pieces that have texture, origin and a story. Genuine cork, harvested by hand in Portugal, answers all three, which is why handcrafted cork art is finding its way into American homes, boutique hotels and design-led offices.

This guide explains why luxury cork decor works so well in American interiors, how to style it, and how to order a handcrafted piece from Portugal to the United States.

## Why designers in the US are choosing genuine cork

**Sustainability that clients can see.** Many American clients now ask for sustainable materials, but they also want them to look luxurious. Cork offers both: it is harvested without cutting down the cork oak, the bark regrows every nine years, and the finished piece shows its natural grain proudly.

**Warmth in large, open spaces.** Open-plan lofts and high-ceilinged living rooms can feel cold. A sculptural cork piece brings warmth, softness and an organic shape that balances concrete, glass and steel.

**One of a kind, never mass-produced.** Every Sildel piece is shaped by hand from a unique piece of bark. In a market full of identical catalogue furniture, that individuality is a true luxury.

## How to style Portuguese cork art in an American home

- **Make it the focal point.** A large sculptural bowl such as Abyss or Equilibrium on a console, dining table or kitchen island draws the eye and starts conversations.
- **Layer warm light.** Cork lighting, such as the Crescent suspension or the Leaf and Shale table lamps, casts a soft, golden glow that suits cosy dens, bedrooms and reading corners.
- **Mix with marble and metal.** Cork pairs naturally with Carrara marble, brushed steel and glass, materials already loved in contemporary American design.
- **Let it breathe.** Give the piece space on a clean surface so its fissures and texture can be appreciated, much like a work of art in a gallery.

## Caring for cork in American homes

Genuine cork is naturally resistant to dust and moisture and needs very little care. Wipe it gently with a dry cloth, avoid chemical cleaning products, and do not fill decorative pieces with liquids. Placed away from prolonged direct sunlight, a cork piece keeps its beauty for generations.
