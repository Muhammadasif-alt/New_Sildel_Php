Some homes are built around things that have lasted: a grandmother's dresser, a clock that has been wound every Sunday for fifty years, books whose spines have faded in the same sunny corner. If you love that kind of home, you probably look at new decor with one question in mind: will it still feel right in twenty years? Genuine Portuguese cork is one of the rare natural materials that does.

## Why people who love old things are drawn to cork

What we admire in antiques is not age alone. It is honest material, the mark of the hand and a little imperfection that tells a story. Cork has all three. It is the bark of the cork oak, stripped by hand from the living tree and left to grow back for about nine years before the next harvest. No two pieces of bark are the same, and the fissures and grain you see on a Sildel piece are the ones the tree made.

Cork also has a long history of everyday use in Portugal, from wine stoppers to the cork vessels that shepherds in the Alentejo carried for generations. Our [Fireflies](/treasures/fireflies) light, for example, was inspired by an old Alentejo custom. A cork piece does not imitate tradition. It comes from it.

## How to pair cork with antiques and family pieces

Cork's warm browns sit naturally beside the materials of a classic home. A few combinations work especially well:

- **Dark wood.** Cork brings a lighter, softer texture next to mahogany, walnut or oak furniture, so the room feels warm rather than heavy.
- **Marble and stone.** Several Sildel pieces, such as [Óscar](/treasures/oscar), rest on white marble. Together they echo the marble tops of old chests of drawers and console tables.
- **Brass, gilt and old mirrors.** The honey tones of cork glow beside brass candlesticks and gilded frames. Our [Halley](/treasures/halley) mirror sets a round mirror in a cork frame that suits a hallway or a bedroom dresser.
- **Linen, wool and leather.** Natural textiles and cork share the same quiet, tactile character.

## Pieces made to be passed down

The best compliment for an old piece of furniture is that someone wanted to keep it. We make our pieces with that in mind: by hand, from a material that is light, resistant to dust and that ages gracefully. With simple care, they can move from your home to your children's.

Caring for cork is easy:

- Dust with a dry, soft cloth.
- If anything is spilt, absorb it at once with a dry cloth.
- Avoid chemical cleaning products.
- Clean marble bases with a cloth dampened with water, then dry and polish with a chamois.
